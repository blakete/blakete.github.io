SCARLUS is a mnemonic I came up with a few years ago for the seven principles of influence from Robert Cialdini's research: Scarcity, Consistency, Authority, Reciprocity, Likeability, Unity, and Social proof. Each one describes a shortcut people use to decide, and each can be applied ethically to persuade.

## Scarcity

People want more of what is rare or dwindling. Perceived scarcity raises perceived value.

Example: "Only 3 spots left in the cohort" drives sign-ups far harder than "spots available."

## Consistency

People strive to act in line with their prior commitments and self-image. Small commitments pave the way for larger ones.

Example: Someone who publicly agrees a cause matters is far more likely to later donate to it.

## Authority

People defer to credible experts and legitimate signals of expertise. Credentials, track record, and confidence all carry weight.

Example: "Recommended by 9 out of 10 dentists" moves product because dentists are the authority on teeth.

## Reciprocity

People feel obligated to return favors, gifts, and concessions. Give first, and give something genuinely valuable.

Example: A free sample or free useful advice makes the recipient far more likely to buy or help later.

## Likeability

People say yes to those they like. Similarity, genuine compliments, and cooperation build liking fast.

Example: A salesperson who finds real common ground with a customer closes more deals than one who goes straight to the pitch.

## Unity

People are swayed by those they share an identity with. "One of us" is more powerful than "one like us."

Example: "As fellow MIT founders, we should stick together" lands differently than any appeal from an outsider.

## Social Proof

People look to what others are doing to decide what is correct, especially under uncertainty. The more similar the others, the stronger the pull.

Example: "Most guests reuse their towels" outperforms environmental appeals in getting hotel guests to reuse theirs.
